# Synthetic Case: Modular Wall-Mounted Storage Rail

This case is synthetic and is included to demonstrate the method. It does not identify a real brand, seller, patent, supplier, or legal conclusion.

## Brief

A cross-border home-organization team is preparing a modular wall-mounted storage rail. The target product uses a long rounded rail, two identical suspended baskets, a centered decorative bridge, and repeated horizontal openings. The team wants to retain load capacity, one-tool installation, and a low landed cost while reducing the chance of looking substantially the same as a serious registered-design candidate.

## Search Plan

The search uses:

- product and category synonyms for storage rail, wall organizer, basket rail, and modular shelf;
- full-product and local-feature image searches for the rail end, basket bracket, and opening pattern;
- Locarno and national design classifications for storage and household fixtures;
- applicant, assignee, designer, supplier, and portfolio searches;
- family, citation, and archived product-page checks.

The report logs the cutoff date and treats any candidate found only through an aggregator as unverified until an official drawing set is located.

## Scope Finding

The serious candidate is not summarized as “a rail with two baskets.” Its relevant visual arrangement is described as a rounded continuous body, equal suspended modules, a centered bridge, a specific end return, and a repeated opening rhythm shown across the claimed views. The target's different coating color is given little weight. The end view and the relationship between the rail and baskets carry more comparison value.

## Risk Split

- **Substantive infringement:** medium before a second-pass comparison because the overall rail-plus-suspended-module silhouette is close.
- **Marketplace complaint:** medium-high if the candidate is active and the rights holder monitors household organizers.
- **Ownership uncertainty:** medium because the listing names a trading company but not the factory or designer.
- **Unpublished-application risk:** unresolved until the launch and filing timeline is checked with counsel.

These are separate conclusions, not one blended score.

## Three Design-Around Directions

1. **Planar spine with integrated pockets**: replace the continuous rounded rail and suspended baskets with a folded plate spine and recessed, unequal storage pockets. The visual rhythm changes from repeated hanging modules to integrated zones.
2. **Segmented vertical modules**: use three independent wall plates with offset containers, no center bridge, and different front/rear proportions. This preserves capacity while breaking the continuous-body silhouette.
3. **Cantilevered blade shelves**: use two thin blade-like shelves with hidden rear supports, closed ends, and a non-repeating opening pattern confined to the shelf recesses.

The preferred direction is the planar spine because it changes the primary silhouette, component relationship, opening rhythm, and end treatment together while keeping the same installation points.

## Prototype And Cost-Down Gates

The preferred concept must pass:

- load and pull-out testing at the required wall anchors;
- packaging and flat-pack assembly review;
- supplier confirmation that the recessed pockets can be formed without restoring the avoided rounded rail;
- a second-pass search using the new terms planar spine, integrated pocket, folded plate organizer, and recessed shelf module;
- an engineering sign-off that the center bridge, equal hanging baskets, and repeated horizontal openings are not reintroduced for cost or tooling reasons.

## Lesson

Changing the rail coating or adding a logo would not be a credible design-around. The stronger approach changes several high-salience visual relationships, preserves the required job-to-be-done, tests manufacturing feasibility, and searches the redesign again before release.
